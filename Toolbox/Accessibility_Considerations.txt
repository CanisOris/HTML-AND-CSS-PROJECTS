Structure & Semantics

Define a single <h1> per page to establish the core topic for assistive technologies.

Nest headings sequentially (<h2> to <h6>) without skipping levels to maintain a logical document outline.

Eliminate empty anchor tags or buttons that create silent, confusing tab-stops for screen readers.


Forms & Inputs

Pair every input, textarea, and select menu with a programmatic label (using <label for="..."> linked to an id, or an aria-label).

Avoid relying on placeholder attributes as the sole identifier for form fields, as they frequently disappear on focus and are often ignored by screen readers.


Keyboard Navigation

Ensure every interactive element is reachable and operable via the keyboard alone (Tab, Enter, Space).

Define explicit :focus-visible CSS states, particularly for custom UI components (like hidden default checkboxes) where native browser focus rings are overridden.


Media & SVGs

Write alt text that conveys the functional purpose or informational content of an image rather than describing visual aesthetics.

Apply aria-hidden="true" to purely decorative SVGs or icons to prevent screen readers from announcing raw geometric shapes out of context.

Omit autoplay attributes on video iframes and media elements to prevent disruptive audio or motion, respecting cognitive accessibility guidelines.

Add descriptive title attributes to all <iframe> elements to explicitly announce the embedded content.


Color & Contrast

Maintain a minimum WCAG AA contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for standard text against its background container.
